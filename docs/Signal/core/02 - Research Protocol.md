# Signal - Research Protocol

This is the default protocol for deep market analysis.

## 1. Identify the Market

Record:

- question;
- condition id;
- current YES/NO price;
- best bid/ask if available;
- volume and liquidity;
- end date;
- vertical;
- Polymarket URL.

## 2. Read Resolution Criteria

Before forecasting, answer:

- What exactly must happen for YES?
- What exactly makes NO win?
- Who or what is the resolution source?
- Is there ambiguous wording?
- Could the market be cancelled or disputed?

If criteria are unclear, confidence should be capped.

## 3. Build the Base Rate

Ask:

- How often do similar events happen?
- Is this a common event dressed up as rare?
- Is this a rare event dressed up as likely?
- What reference class is most honest?

Base rate is the anchor. News modifies it.

## 4. Gather Evidence

Prefer:

- official statements;
- primary documents;
- direct data;
- high-quality reporting;
- multiple independent sources.

Track each source as:

- supports YES;
- supports NO;
- neutral/context;
- outdated;
- low reliability.

## 5. Write the YES Case

Make the strongest honest case that YES is underpriced.

Include:

- evidence;
- mechanism;
- timeline;
- catalysts;
- what the market may be missing.

## 6. Write the NO Case

Make the strongest honest case that the market is right or YES is overpriced.

Include:

- blocking conditions;
- incentives against the event;
- structural reasons;
- resolution traps;
- base-rate pushback.

## 7. Assign Probability

Estimate:

- probability_yes;
- confidence;
- expected direction;
- largest uncertainty.

The number should be calibrated. A boring 56% can be better than a dramatic 75%.

## 8. Trading Decision

A signal requires:

- estimated edge above threshold;
- confidence above threshold;
- spread-adjusted edge positive;
- clear resolution criteria;
- enough liquidity;
- no unresolved contradiction in evidence.

No-signal reasons should be explicit:

- edge below threshold;
- confidence too low;
- spread too wide;
- liquidity too thin;
- resolution ambiguity;
- already priced in;
- insufficient evidence.

## 9. Post-Resolution Review

After the market resolves, write:

- what happened;
- whether reasoning was correct;
- whether the forecast was calibrated;
- whether the signal made money;
- what should change in future forecasts.
